# A380 Short: fact sources

- Airbus A380 (Wikipedia, citing Airbus): first flight 27 April 2005; entry into service 25 October 2007 with Singapore Airlines (SQ380);
  production end announced February 2019; 251 delivered, the last (MSN 272, A6-EVS) to Emirates on 16 December 2021;
  Emirates received 123 (its 123rd was the 251st and last); 116 in service with Emirates as of May 2025; 189 in service worldwide.
  A380-800: length 72.72 m, wingspan 79.75 m, height 24.09 m; typical 525 seats (3-class), certified for 853.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380
- Boeing 777-300ER: length 73.86 m (242 ft 4 in), wingspan 64.80 m (212 ft 7 in), height 18.5 m.
  Boeing "777-200LR/-300ER/-Freighter Airplane Characteristics for Airport Planning" (Rev G):
  https://www.boeing.com/resources/boeingdotcom/commercial/airports/acaps/777-200LR-300ER-F_Rev_G.pdf ; https://dimensions.com/element/boeing-777-300er
- Why it ended: airlines shifted to point-to-point routes; twin-engine widebodies (787, A350, 777) offer similar range with lower trip cost
  and better fuel efficiency; orders dried up (Wikipedia "Airbus A380", Production end / Market sections).

Derived numbers in the VO: "a metre shorter" = 73.86 - 72.72 = 1.14 m; "fifteen metres wider" = 79.75 - 64.80 = 14.95 m;
"almost half" = 123 / 251 = 49 %. "Over five hundred seats" = 525 typical.
